1 Introduction

This analysis develops a sample design for estimating transit submode shares within Purpose × Auto Ownership segments. The design balances three considerations: the number of segments receiving a formal precision guarantee, the margin-of-error target for those segments, and the total number of completed surveys required.

Because some Purpose × Auto Ownership segments represent a very small share of transit trips, requiring the same precision for all segments can result in very large sample requirements. The analysis therefore evaluates alternative incidence thresholds that determine which segments must meet a ±3 or ±5 percentage-point margin-of-error target. Segments below the selected threshold remain represented in the survey but do not drive the total sample requirement.

The resulting scenarios show the tradeoff between segment coverage, statistical precision, and total sample size, providing a basis for selecting an appropriate survey design.

2 Analysis structure

The previous tour mode choice calibration considers six trip purposes—Work, University, School, Individual Maintenance, Individual Discretionary, and Individual At-Work—and three auto-ownership categories: Autos = 0, Autos < Workers, and Autos >= Workers. Together, these define 18 Purpose × Auto Ownership segments.

Within each segment, the survey estimates the distribution across eight calibration transit modes: Local, LRT-Walk, LRT-Drive, Ferry-Walk, Ferry-Drive, Express Bus, Heavy Rail, and Commuter Rail. This produces up to 144 calibration analysis domains (18 segments × 8 modes).

3 Terminology

A segment is a combination of Purpose × Auto Ownership. For example, Work × Autos = 0 is one segment.

A domain is a particular calibration-mode share within a segment. For example,

\[ P(\text{LRT-Walk}\mid\text{Work},\text{Autos}=0) \]

is one detailed analysis domain.

The formal precision requirement is applied at the segment level: qualifying segments must have enough expected completed surveys to support the specified margin-of-error target for calibration-mode shares within that segment.

4 Sampling strata versus analysis domains

Purpose, Auto Ownership, and access mode cannot be observed before interviewing a rider; they are learned from the questionnaire. The operational sampling design therefore combines the calibration modes into six sampling-mode strata:

  1. Local
  2. LRT
  3. Ferry
  4. Express Bus
  5. Heavy Rail
  6. Commuter Rail

5 Role of the historical distribution

The historical weighted trip distribution is used as a planning basis for the expected composition of the survey. Specifically, it provides the expected prevalence of each Purpose × Auto Ownership segment and the expected distribution of completed surveys across the six sampling-mode strata.

The required number of completes within each qualifying segment is based on the conservative assumption of (p=0.50), so the segment-level precision requirement does not depend on historical calibration-mode shares being reproduced in the new survey. The historical distribution is used only for sample planning.

However, since historical segment incidence is used to translate the required number of completes within each qualifying segment into an overall survey sample requirement, the resulting total sample assumes that the new survey will have a segment composition similar to the historical distribution. If a qualifying segment occurs less frequently than expected, the planned total sample may yield fewer than the required segment-level completes, and the target margin of error may not be achieved for that segment.

The travel model represents typical weekday travel, defined as Tuesday through Thursday. Accordingly, the total survey completes identified in this analysis are intended to be collected on those days. The historical distribution used for sample planning has a slightly broader weekday definition, covering Monday through Friday. This difference cannot be reconciled at this time and is therefore treated as a limitation of the sample-planning analysis.

6 Analysis workflow

The analysis proceeds from statistical precision requirements to an operational sampling plan:

  1. Define analysis domains. Establish 18 segments × eight calibration transit modes.
  2. Set the precision standard. Specify the margin-of-error scenarios and calculate the required completes per qualifying segment.
  3. Build the historical distribution. Read the previous model calibration data, then calculate each segment and calibration transit mode’s share of total transit ridership.
  4. Evaluate incidence thresholds. Determine which segments qualify under each incidence threshold.
  5. Calculate total survey requirements. For each threshold and margin-of-error scenario, identify the least prevalent qualifying segment. This binding segment determines the required overall sample: \[ N=\left\lceil\frac{\text{required segment completes}}{\text{binding segment incidence}}\right\rceil. \]
  6. Allocate completes proportionally. Distribute each scenario’s total sample across the 144 segment × calibration-mode cells using the historical distribution.
  7. Translate to operational sampling strata. Aggregate the eight calibration transit modes into six sampling-mode strata that can be controlled during data collection.
  8. Apply sampling-mode minimums. Raise any sampling-mode stratum below the relevant minimum, increasing the total survey size where necessary.
  9. Evaluate achieved precision. Redistribute the adjusted sampling-mode totals to segments and the eight calibration transit modes, then calculate the expected margin of error for every calibration-mode share.

7.1 Define the analysis domains

The analysis evaluates calibration-mode shares within each Purpose × Auto Ownership segment using the framework defined in Section 2. The precision requirement is applied at the segment level, rather than independently to each calibration-mode domain. The following steps determine which segments receive a formal precision guarantee and the total survey sample needed to achieve it.

7.2 Set the precision standard

For a proportion under simple random sampling, the approximate 95% margin of error is

\[ MOE=1.96\sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}{n}}. \]

Solving for sample size gives

\[ n=\frac{1.96^2p(1-p)}{MOE^2}. \]

The sample-size calculation for a proportion requires an assumed calibration-mode share, (p). Rather than relying on historical calibration-mode shares, this analysis uses (p=0.50) for all domains. As a result, the sample-size requirement does not depend on the historical calibration-mode shares being correct or remaining stable in the new survey.

Because the variance of a proportion, (p(1-p)), is maximized at (p=0.50), this provides a conservative basis for determining the sample size needed to meet the specified margin-of-error target.

For ±3 percentage points,

\[ n=\frac{1.96^2(0.50)(0.50)}{0.03^2}=1067.11, \]

which is rounded upward to 1,068 completes per qualifying segment.

For ±5 percentage points,

\[ n=\frac{1.96^2(0.50)(0.50)}{0.05^2}=384.15, \]

which is rounded upward to 385 completes per qualifying segment.

Margin of Error Confidence Conservative \(p\) Required Segment \(n\)
±3 percentage points 95% 0.50 1,068
±5 percentage points 95% 0.50 385

7.9 Evaluate achieved precision

After applying the sampling-mode minimums, expected precision is evaluated for all 144 domains, including segments below the incidence threshold that are not subject to the formal precision requirement. This evaluation shows the precision those segments are nevertheless expected to achieve under the resulting survey design.

The adjusted sampling-mode total is distributed across its associated segment × calibration-mode cells in proportion to the historical distribution within that sampling-mode stratum. The resulting expected completes are summed within each segment. For calibration transit mode \(d\) in segment \(j\), the approximate 95% margin of error is

\[ MOE_{jd}=1.96\sqrt{\frac{p_{jd}(1-p_{jd})}{n_j}}, \]

where \(n_j\) is the segment’s expected adjusted completes and \(p_{jd}\) is the calibration transit mode’s expected share within that segment.

Each cell reports share (±MOE), where the value in parentheses is the margin of error. The share is the mode’s expected percentage within that segment after applying the sampling-mode adjustments.

These margin-of-error calculations assume simple random sampling. In practice, the survey will be collected across specific routes, stops, and time periods and may also use survey weights. These features can reduce the effective statistical precision of the sample. Therefore, the calculated sample sizes should be interpreted as planning targets rather than guarantees of the final margin of error.

# Introduction

This analysis develops a sample design for estimating transit submode shares within **Purpose × Auto Ownership segments**. The design balances three considerations: the number of segments receiving a formal precision guarantee, the margin-of-error target for those segments, and the total number of completed surveys required.

Because some Purpose × Auto Ownership segments represent a very small share of transit trips, requiring the same precision for all segments can result in very large sample requirements. The analysis therefore evaluates alternative **incidence thresholds** that determine which segments must meet a ±3 or ±5 percentage-point margin-of-error target. Segments below the selected threshold remain represented in the survey but do not drive the total sample requirement.

The resulting scenarios show the tradeoff between **segment coverage, statistical precision, and total sample size**, providing a basis for selecting an appropriate survey design.

# Analysis structure

The previous tour mode choice calibration considers six trip purposes—Work, University, School, Individual Maintenance, Individual Discretionary, and Individual At-Work—and three auto-ownership categories: Autos = 0, Autos < Workers, and Autos >= Workers. Together, these define **18 Purpose × Auto Ownership segments**.

Within each segment, the survey estimates the distribution across eight calibration transit modes: Local, LRT-Walk, LRT-Drive, Ferry-Walk, Ferry-Drive, Express Bus, Heavy Rail, and Commuter Rail. This produces up to **144 calibration analysis domains** (18 segments × 8 modes).

# Terminology

A **segment** is a combination of Purpose × Auto Ownership. For example, `Work × Autos = 0` is one segment.

A **domain** is a particular calibration-mode share within a segment. For example,

$$
P(\text{LRT-Walk}\mid\text{Work},\text{Autos}=0)
$$

is one detailed analysis domain.

The formal precision requirement is applied at the segment level: qualifying segments must have enough expected completed surveys to support the specified margin-of-error target for calibration-mode shares within that segment.

# Sampling strata versus analysis domains

Purpose, Auto Ownership, and access mode cannot be observed before interviewing a rider; they are learned from the questionnaire. The operational sampling design therefore combines the calibration modes into six sampling-mode strata:

1. Local
2. LRT
3. Ferry
4. Express Bus
5. Heavy Rail
6. Commuter Rail

# Role of the historical distribution

The historical weighted trip distribution is used as a planning basis for the expected composition of the survey. Specifically, it provides the expected prevalence of each Purpose × Auto Ownership segment and the expected distribution of completed surveys across the six sampling-mode strata.

The required number of completes within each qualifying segment is based on the conservative assumption of (p=0.50), so the segment-level precision requirement does not depend on historical calibration-mode shares being reproduced in the new survey. The historical distribution is used only for sample planning.

However, since historical segment incidence is used to translate the required number of completes within each qualifying segment into an overall survey sample requirement, the resulting total sample assumes that the new survey will have a segment composition similar to the historical distribution. If a qualifying segment occurs less frequently than expected, the planned total sample may yield fewer than the required segment-level completes, and the target margin of error may not be achieved for that segment.

The travel model represents typical weekday travel, defined as Tuesday through Thursday. Accordingly, the total survey completes identified in this analysis are intended to be collected on those days. The historical distribution used for sample planning has a slightly broader weekday definition, covering Monday through Friday. This difference cannot be reconciled at this time and is therefore treated as a limitation of the sample-planning analysis.


# Analysis workflow

The analysis proceeds from statistical precision requirements to an operational sampling plan:

1. **Define analysis domains.** Establish 18 segments × eight calibration transit modes.
2. **Set the precision standard.** Specify the margin-of-error scenarios and calculate the required completes per qualifying segment.
3. **Build the historical distribution.** Read the previous model calibration data, then calculate each segment and calibration transit mode's share of total transit ridership.
4. **Evaluate incidence thresholds.** Determine which segments qualify under each incidence threshold.
5. **Calculate total survey requirements.** For each threshold and margin-of-error scenario, identify the least prevalent qualifying segment. This binding segment determines the required overall sample:
  $$
  N=\left\lceil\frac{\text{required segment completes}}{\text{binding segment incidence}}\right\rceil.
  $$
6. **Allocate completes proportionally.** Distribute each scenario's total sample across the 144 segment × calibration-mode cells using the historical distribution.
7. **Translate to operational sampling strata.** Aggregate the eight calibration transit modes into six sampling-mode strata that can be controlled during data collection.
8. **Apply sampling-mode minimums.** Raise any sampling-mode stratum below the relevant minimum, increasing the total survey size where necessary.
9. **Evaluate achieved precision.** Redistribute the adjusted sampling-mode totals to segments and the eight calibration transit modes, then calculate the expected margin of error for every calibration-mode share.

## Define the analysis domains
The analysis evaluates calibration-mode shares within each Purpose × Auto Ownership segment using the framework defined in Section 2. The precision requirement is applied at the segment level, rather than independently to each calibration-mode domain. ## Set the precision standard

For a proportion under simple random sampling, the approximate 95% margin of error is

$$
MOE=1.96\sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}{n}}.
$$

Solving for sample size gives

$$
n=\frac{1.96^2p(1-p)}{MOE^2}.
$$

The sample-size calculation for a proportion requires an assumed calibration-mode share, (p). Rather than relying on historical calibration-mode shares, this analysis uses (p=0.50) for all domains. As a result, the sample-size requirement does not depend on the historical calibration-mode shares being correct or remaining stable in the new survey.

Because the variance of a proportion, (p(1-p)), is maximized at (p=0.50), this provides a conservative basis for determining the sample size needed to meet the specified margin-of-error target.

For ±3 percentage points,

$$
n=\frac{1.96^2(0.50)(0.50)}{0.03^2}=1067.11,
$$

which is rounded upward to **1,068 completes per qualifying segment**.

For ±5 percentage points,

$$
n=\frac{1.96^2(0.50)(0.50)}{0.05^2}=384.15,
$$

which is rounded upward to **385 completes per qualifying segment**.

| Margin of Error | Confidence | Conservative $p$ | Required Segment $n$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ±3 percentage points | 95% | 0.50 | 1,068 |
| ±5 percentage points | 95% | 0.50 | 385 |

## Evaluate achieved precision

After applying the sampling-mode minimums, expected precision is evaluated for all 144 domains, including segments below the incidence threshold that are not subject to the formal precision requirement. This evaluation shows the precision those segments are nevertheless expected to achieve under the resulting survey design.

The adjusted sampling-mode total is distributed across its associated segment × calibration-mode cells in proportion to the historical distribution within that sampling-mode stratum. The resulting expected completes are summed within each segment. For calibration transit mode $d$ in segment $j$, the approximate 95% margin of error is

$$
MOE_{jd}=1.96\sqrt{\frac{p_{jd}(1-p_{jd})}{n_j}},
$$

where $n_j$ is the segment's expected adjusted completes and $p_{jd}$ is the calibration transit mode's expected share within that segment.

Each cell reports `share (±MOE)`, where the value in parentheses is the margin of error. The share is the mode's expected percentage within that segment after applying the sampling-mode adjustments.

These margin-of-error calculations assume simple random sampling. In practice, the survey will be collected across specific routes, stops, and time periods and may also use survey weights. These features can reduce the effective statistical precision of the sample. Therefore, the calculated sample sizes should be interpreted as planning targets rather than guarantees of the final margin of error.

```{python}
#| label: adjusted-detailed-mode-moe
#| echo: false
CALIBRATION_TO_SAMPLING = {
  "Local": "Local",
  "LRT-Walk": "LRT",
  "LRT-Drive": "LRT",
  "Ferry-Walk": "Ferry",
  "Ferry-Drive": "Ferry",
  "Express Bus": "Express Bus",
  "Heavy Rail": "Heavy Rail",
  "Commuter Rail": "Commuter Rail",
}
adjusted_expected_allocations = {}
adjusted_moe_tables = {}

for threshold_label, floor_table in mode_floor_tables.items():
  for moe_label in MODE_FLOORS:
    adjusted_mode_totals = adjusted_mode_totals_by_scenario[
      (threshold_label, moe_label)
    ]
    expected_allocation = pd.DataFrame(
      0.0,
      index=distribution_shares.index,
      columns=DETAILED_MODES,
    )

    for sampling_mode in sampling_modes:
      detailed_columns = [
        mode
        for mode, parent_mode in CALIBRATION_TO_SAMPLING.items()
        if parent_mode == sampling_mode
      ]
      historical_cells = distribution_shares[detailed_columns]
      conditional_distribution = (
        historical_cells / historical_cells.to_numpy().sum()
      )
      expected_allocation[detailed_columns] = (
        conditional_distribution
        * float(adjusted_mode_totals[sampling_mode])
      )

      if not np.isclose(
        expected_allocation[detailed_columns].to_numpy().sum(),
        adjusted_mode_totals[sampling_mode],
      ):
        raise RuntimeError(
          f"Expected allocations do not reproduce the {sampling_mode} total."
        )

    segment_completes = expected_allocation.sum(axis="columns")
    within_segment_shares = expected_allocation.div(
      segment_completes,
      axis="index",
    )
    detailed_moe = 1.96 * np.sqrt(
      within_segment_shares
      * (1 - within_segment_shares)
      / segment_completes.to_numpy()[:, None]
    )

    share_moe_table = pd.DataFrame(index=within_segment_shares.index)
    for mode in DETAILED_MODES:
      share_moe_table[mode] = [
        f"{share:.2%} (±{moe:.2%})"
        for share, moe in zip(
          within_segment_shares[mode],
          detailed_moe[mode],
        )
      ]
    share_moe_table = share_moe_table.reset_index()
    share_moe_table.insert(
      3,
      "Expected Segment Completes",
      segment_completes.to_numpy(),
    )

    scenario_name = (
      f"Incidence threshold: {threshold_label}; MOE target: {moe_label}"
    )
    adjusted_expected_allocations[scenario_name] = expected_allocation
    adjusted_moe_tables[scenario_name] = share_moe_table

    if is_worked_example(threshold_label, moe_label):
      display(Markdown(
        f"### Worked example: {WORKED_EXAMPLE_LABEL} — "
        f"{int(adjusted_mode_totals['Total Completes']):,} "
        "adjusted completes"
      ))
      table_caption("Worked example — expected precision by calibration transit mode.")
      display(share_moe_table.style.format({
        "Expected Segment Completes": "{:,.1f}",
      }).set_table_attributes(
        'class="eight-mode-moe-table"'
      ).hide(axis="index").hide(
        axis="columns",
        subset=["Purpose Number"],
      ))

  raise RuntimeError(
    f"Expected {expected_scenario_count} adjusted MOE tables; "
```

# Conclusion

The analysis illustrates the tradeoff between **segment coverage, statistical precision, and total sample size**. Extending a common precision standard to lower-incidence segments can substantially increase the required overall sample.

The scenarios presented here provide a range of possible survey designs. The table in Section 7.8.2 summarizes the coverage–precision–sample-size tradeoff across the alternative scenarios. For example, a 3% incidence threshold with a ±3 percentage-point target provides the specified precision for 9 qualifying segments, which together represent close to 90% of historical weighted transit trips. This scenario requires 33,074 completes under the proportional design, increasing to 34,166 completes after applying the operational sampling-mode minimums. The use of an incidence threshold helps avoid the substantially larger sample sizes required to extend that standard to lower-incidence segments. Other scenarios may be appropriate depending on survey budget and other priorities.

The final design should therefore be selected based on the desired level of segment coverage and precision, together with the total sample size that is feasible to collect. 

Whichever design is selected, the resulting sample target is intended to be achieved through Tuesday-through-Thursday data collection, consistent with the travel model’s representation of typical weekday travel.
